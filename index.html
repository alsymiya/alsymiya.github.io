01 / About

A bit about me

I earned my PhD in Mechanical Engineering at Stony Brook University in May 2026, with a minor in Computer Science. I trained in the Computer-Aided Design and Innovation Lab under Prof. Anurag Purwar.

My research connects three threads: data-driven design, algebraic graph theory for kinematic structure, and algorithm engineering for real-time CAD. My core focus is simulation speed — faster simulation keeps interactive design tools responsive for end users, and is equally critical for mass-producing the datasets that machine-learning pipelines depend on.

I have four first-author journal papers in the ASME Journal of Mechanical Design and Journal of Mechanisms and Robotics, with one selected as a featured article.

02 / Projects

Software & Research Projects

Where research meets practice — code that ships to real users.

Flagship · 2019 — Present

MotionGen

Web-based CAD application for planar mechanism design.
Funded by NSF & Mechanismic Inc.

110,000 registered users (May 2026)

  • Display — Implemented customizable shape elements on the canvas using paper.js (2019–2020).
  • Mechanism Simulation — Built a unified, generalizable, differentiable, efficient planar linkage simulator using approximation (2022); extended capability via a Pebble Game framework for gears and wheels (2024–2025).
  • Mechanism Synthesis — Deployed G-manifold algebraic fitting for motion synthesis (2022); backend on Google Cloud with a neural-network pipeline (2023); compact data structure cutting storage to 1/100 of original with custom topology dictionary (2024); 3M-sample dataset (2024); differential evolution for synthesis (2025).
  • Ongoing (2025–2026) — Vectorization of the simulator for real-time synthesis; differentiable physics for synthesis fine-tuning; multibody dynamics for real-time rendering.

Sharp user-growth spike in August 2023 after the popular mechanism-design YouTube channel Engineezy used MotionGen to design a prototype.

Visit motiongen.io
Algorithms · 2024 — 2025

Extended Pebble Game Framework

Polynomial-time mobility analysis for planar mechanisms with linkages, gears, and wheels.

  • Extends classical pebble-game constraint analysis from 2 constraint types to 3 (distance, angle, angle-angle).
  • Python prototype, intended for integration into the next MotionGen simulator.
Machine Learning · 2022 — 2024

Mechanism Synthesis Pipeline (VAE)

Atlas-based, data-driven path synthesis using Variational Auto-Encoders.

  • Sit-to-Stand (STS) ML data preprocessing & path-synthesis backend rewrite (2023) — foundation for the featured ASME-JMD paper.
  • Three-million-sample R/P-joint mechanism dataset.
  • Three ranking metrics for retrieved candidates.
  • Deployed end-to-end in MotionGen via Google Cloud API.
Research · 2025 — Present

Differentiable Physics for Design

Coupling ML with a custom differentiable simulator to land designs in the basin of attraction.

  • Sampling-density study across topologies for reliable initialization.
  • Accepted to ASME IDETC/CIE 2026.

03 / Publications

Selected Publications

Peer-reviewed journal papers and conference work. The first three have been deployed in MotionGen.

2024
First Author

A Unified Real-Time Motion Generation Algorithm for Approximate Position Analysis of Planar N-Bar Mechanisms

Zhijie Lyu, Wei Liao, Anurag Purwar

ASME Journal of Mechanical Design, June 2024, 146(6): 063302 (12 pages)

  • Extended the prismatic-joint approximation trick from textbook special cases to arbitrary combinations of joints.
  • Defined a standardized matrix-set data structure for kinematically simulated samples and analyzed its structural complexity.
  • Documents the core of the 2022–2025 MotionGen simulator.
  • Distilled from the author's M.S. thesis, Real-Time Kinematic Simulation of Planar Linkage Mechanisms (Stony Brook, August 2022) — the thesis contains the longer, original treatment.

Deep Learning Conceptual Design of Sit-to-Stand Parallel Motion Six-Bar Mechanisms

Zhijie Lyu, Anurag Purwar

ASME Journal of Mechanical Design, July 2024, 147(1): 1–18

  • Improved an atlas-based, data-driven approach for planar linkage path synthesis through targeted data preprocessing.

First Author

Point-based Models: A Unified Approach for Geometric Constraint Analysis of Planar N-Bar Mechanisms with Rotary, Sliding, and Rolling Joints

Zhijie Lyu, Anurag Purwar

ASME Journal of Mechanisms and Robotics, Special Issue for IDETC-CIE 2024

  • Developed methodology and data structures that extend the constraint-graph approach from two constraint types (distance, angle) to three (angle-angle), enabling implementation of wheels and gears in linkage systems.
  • Theoretical foundation: wheels can be represented as linkages, allowing all elements to be analyzed like a truss structure.
